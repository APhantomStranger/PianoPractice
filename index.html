Finger numbers & hand position

Each finger has a number, same on both hands: 1 thumb, 2 index, 3 middle, 4 ring, 5 pinky. Sheet music uses these numbers to tell you which finger to use.

Finger12345
NameThumbIndexMiddleRingPinky

Sit tall, feet flat, wrists level with the keys. Curve your fingers as if holding a small ball — play on the pads, not flat.

The white keys

White keys repeat A–G. The trick: C is always just left of any group of two black keys; F is just left of any group of three. Find those two and the rest fall into place.

Reading the Grand Staff

The treble clef (𝄞) is usually your right hand, the bass clef (𝄢) your left. Together they form the Grand Staff.

Treble lines (bottom→top): E G B D F → "Every Good Boy Does Fine."   Treble spaces: F A C E → "FACE."

Bass lines (bottom→top): G B D F A → "Good Boys Do Fine Always."   Bass spaces: A C E G → "All Cows Eat Grass."

Middle C sits on a short ledger line between the two staves — the bridge linking your hands.

C major scale — the first scale

All white keys, no sharps or flats. The challenge is the thumb tuck so you can keep going past five notes.

Note & key recognition drills

Short, repeatable exercises that build the instant recognition reading music depends on. Rotate through a few each day.

1
Landmark notes
Memorize four anchors first: Middle C, Treble G (2nd line), Bass F (4th line), and Treble high C. Find each on the keyboard in under 2 seconds, then read nearby notes by stepping from a landmark.
2
Keyboard geography
Eyes closed, place your hand and find C by feeling for the two-black-key groups. Then find every C up and down the piano, then every F. This locks in your map of the keys.
3
Steps & skips
On the staff, a note on a line whose neighbor is on the next line up is a skip; line-to-adjacent-space is a step. Practice naming "step up / skip down" without naming letters — it trains shape reading.
4
Note-naming sprint
Open the Note Trainer and answer 20 flashcards in a row. Note your accuracy, then try to beat it tomorrow. Add sharps & flats once naturals feel automatic.
5
Reverse find-the-note
Pick a random letter (say A). Play every A on the keyboard, then point to where A sits in both clefs. Going name → key cements what the trainer teaches key → name.
6
Say-as-you-play
In your repertoire block, say each note's letter name out loud as you play it, hands separately. Slow, but it welds the printed note to the key under your finger.
